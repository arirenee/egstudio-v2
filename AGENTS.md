# EG Studio — Codex Instructions

## Mission
Help EG Studio find and validate a stronger, simpler video-editing business model before changing the live website.

Current strategic direction:
- Expand beyond agency-only buyers.
- Consider creators, podcasters, consultants, coaches, founders, SMBs, brands, marketing teams, and agencies.
- Core customer job: turn raw or long-form footage into finished short-form vertical videos.
- "No call required" is a candidate differentiator to validate with evidence, not an assumption to force.
- EG Studio should aim to make pricing, deliverables, turnaround, revisions, process, and checkout understandable without a sales call.

## Commercial priority
The primary goal is a paying customer and a repeatable profitable offer. Revenue/customer evidence outranks internal preference.

When evaluating offers, prioritize:
1. Evidence that buyers already pay for the service.
2. Clear, easy-to-buy packaging.
3. Competitive entry price or better value per deliverable.
4. Healthy fulfillment economics.
5. Fast turnaround and low operational complexity.
6. Strong fit with a no-call/self-serve buying flow.
7. Ability to launch with EG Studio's existing editing capability.

Do not assume the current $300 Starter or $1,500 Sprint must remain unchanged. Treat them as existing baselines until research supports keeping, replacing, or reframing them.

## Research rules
For competitor, pricing, offer, review, or market research:
- Use current public web evidence.
- Record source URLs and observation date.
- Label material statements as Fact, Hypothesis, or Unknown.
- Never invent prices, customers, sales volume, testimonials, guarantees, review sentiment, turnaround, or company performance.
- Distinguish direct evidence from inference.
- Use multiple sources when a claim materially affects the recommendation.
- Prefer competitors with visible offers, pricing, purchase flows, reviews, or substantial customer proof.
- Look for customer complaints and buying friction, especially forced sales calls, hidden pricing, unclear scope, slow turnaround, revision limits, cancellation friction, and inconsistent quality.
- Do not copy proprietary copy, branding, layout, illustrations, or creative assets verbatim. Extract business-model patterns and improve them for EG Studio.

## Offer research output
When asked to research new offers, compare at least:
- Target buyer
- Customer job-to-be-done
- Deliverables
- Entry price
- Effective price per deliverable where calculable
- Subscription vs one-time
- Turnaround
- Revisions
- Source-footage limits
- Onboarding requirements
- Call required vs self-serve
- Refund/cancellation terms
- Guarantees/risk reversal
- Proof/trust signals
- Upsells
- Customer complaint signals
- Operational complexity
- Likely margin pressure
- Source URLs
- Fact / Hypothesis / Unknown status

Finish research with:
1. The strongest market pattern.
2. The biggest exploitable customer-friction gap.
3. Three concrete EG Studio offer candidates.
4. A scored comparison of those three.
5. One recommended offer to test first.
6. The exact evidence that would falsify the recommendation.

## Change-control rules
Research first. Do not edit the customer-facing website merely because an idea sounds better.

Unless the user explicitly approves implementation:
- Do not modify HTML/CSS/JS, checkout links, pricing, Stripe, onboarding, analytics, legal pages, DNS, or deployment settings.
- Do not merge or deploy.
- Do not deactivate existing offers.
- Do not claim a test has passed unless it was actually run.

For any approved site implementation:
- Work on a branch.
- Preserve existing working functionality unless the approved change requires otherwise.
- Show the proposed diff and test results before merge/deployment.

## Decision standard
Do not recommend a major pivot from one anecdote. Prefer converging evidence from competitor packaging, public pricing, reviews/customer complaints, search demand, and EG Studio's fulfillment economics.

When evidence is weak, say Unknown and propose the cheapest test that can produce customer evidence.
